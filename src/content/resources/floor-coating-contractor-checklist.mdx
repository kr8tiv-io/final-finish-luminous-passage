Use this checklist to make bids comparable. It does not tell you which contractor to hire; it reveals whether each contractor has priced the same floor, preparation, system, safety obligations, and hand-off.

For local scope options, see our [commercial epoxy flooring](/services/commercial-epoxy-flooring).

## Before inviting bids

- [ ] Write the room use, traffic, chemicals, cleaning method, wet conditions, texture priorities, and appearance expectations.
- [ ] Mark cracks, joints, spalls, drains, slopes, edges, old coatings, oil, damp areas, and fixed equipment on a floor plan.
- [ ] State access hours, available power, water, heating, ventilation control, storage, and waste restrictions.
- [ ] Identify the owner representative who can approve hidden-condition changes.
- [ ] Give every bidder the same photos, floor plan, service conditions, and required bid format.

Do not begin with a desired resin name alone. A product can only be evaluated against the substrate and use.

## Contractor and project administration

- [ ] Legal business name and contact information appear on the proposal.
- [ ] The proposal identifies who supervises the work and who performs specialized testing.
- [ ] Required licensing, insurance, permits, and workplace documentation are verified for the actual project—not assumed from a logo.
- [ ] Subcontracted preparation, testing, repairs, electrical work, or waste handling is disclosed.
- [ ] The owner and contractor roles for clearing, utilities, HVAC, security, shutdowns, and emergency access are explicit.
- [ ] Change authorization requires a written scope, price or method, and schedule effect.

For commercial projects, consult the official Alberta OHS Act, Regulation, and Code and assign project responsibilities with competent advice ([Alberta OHS legislation](https://www.alberta.ca/ohs-act-regulation-code)). Do not treat this website checklist as a compliance review.

## Assessment and preparation scope

- [ ] Concrete condition is assessed across representative areas, including edges and beneath movable items.
- [ ] Existing coatings, adhesives, sealers, curing compounds, and contamination are identified or listed as unknowns.
- [ ] Moisture method, locations, timing, results, and product acceptance limits are stated.
- [ ] Crack, spall, weak-concrete, joint, and repair treatment is itemized.
- [ ] The mechanical preparation method and target surface profile are named.
- [ ] Edges, drains, stairs, columns, coves, thresholds, and inaccessible areas have a defined detail.
- [ ] Dust collection, isolation, cleanup, and prepared-surface inspection are included.
- [ ] A hold point occurs after preparation and before primer.

ICRI 310.2R explains that preparation methods have different capabilities, operating requirements, limitations, and resulting profiles ([ICRI 310.2R](https://store.icri.org/item/3102r2013-english-selecting-concrete-surface-preparation-sealers-coatings-polymer-overlays-concrete-repair-342520)). “Diamond grind as needed” is therefore less useful than a method, target, edge plan, and verification procedure.

## Materials and system build

- [ ] Every layer is named by manufacturer and product: repair, primer, body coat, aggregate or flake, grout coat, and topcoat.
- [ ] Colour, sheen, broadcast density, texture, and sample or mock-up approval are documented.
- [ ] Coverage or thickness is stated in the manufacturer’s applicable terms.
- [ ] Product substitutions require written owner approval and renewed compatibility review.
- [ ] Current product data sheets and Canadian SDSs are attached.
- [ ] The contractor states which document governs moisture, temperature, humidity, dew point, mixing, pot life, recoat, and cure.
- [ ] Treatment at intentional joints and moving cracks is explicit.
- [ ] Chemical, UV, tyre, impact, and slip claims are tied to written product/system data and actual use.

CCOHS explains that SDSs communicate hazard identification, first aid, accidental-release measures, handling, exposure controls, properties, and other essential information ([CCOHS SDS guide](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/chemicals/whmis_ghs/sds.html)). An SDS addresses safety; it does not replace the product data sheet or system specification.

## Site logistics and safety coordination

- [ ] Work-zone barriers, signs, authorized access, and adjacent occupant notification are planned.
- [ ] Dust, odour, noise, electrical loads, cords, hoses, and waste routes are addressed.
- [ ] HVAC isolation, exhaust discharge, and replacement-air paths are agreed with the building representative.
- [ ] Material delivery, heated or secure storage, spill response, and mixed-product disposal are assigned.
- [ ] Other trades and live operations are coordinated.
- [ ] Emergency exits, fire equipment, panels, shutoffs, and drains remain appropriately accessible.
- [ ] Contingencies cover power loss, unsuitable conditions, unexpected moisture, and hidden deterioration.

The contractor’s safe-work plan should fit the actual site. Do not ask for a generic template merely to tick a box; ask who reviewed it after the site walk.

## Schedule and inspection gates

- [ ] The schedule separates clearing, preparation, repairs, layers, restricted cure, and recommissioning.
- [ ] Product-specific minimum and maximum recoat windows are mapped to shifts.
- [ ] Required air, slab, material, humidity, and condensation conditions are logged.
- [ ] There are named stop/go decisions before repair concealment, primer, each layer, traffic, and reopening.
- [ ] Traffic classes are separate: worker access, owner inspection, foot traffic, equipment, vehicles, washing, and normal service.
- [ ] Delays caused by conditions have a written decision path.

Reject generic phrases such as “ready next day” unless the bid states ready for what, under which measured conditions, and according to which product document.

## Commercial terms and exclusions

- [ ] Tax, mobilization, equipment moves, utilities, protection, waste, and cleanup are included or clearly excluded.
- [ ] Repair quantity and the unit rate or allowance for concealed damage are stated.
- [ ] Payment milestones follow observable deliverables.
- [ ] Warranty language identifies the provider, duration, covered defects, exclusions, maintenance duties, and claim process.
- [ ] No promise depends on an unverified assumption such as a dry slab or intact vapour retarder.
- [ ] Cancellation, delay, and owner-caused access changes are addressed.

Do not infer credentials, warranties, or performance from marketing language. Verify documents directly and keep copies with the signed agreement.

## Closeout package

- [ ] Final product names, colours, lots, and SDS/PDS versions are supplied.
- [ ] Moisture, preparation, repair, environmental, batch, and inspection records are delivered.
- [ ] Photographs show the prepared floor and concealed repairs.
- [ ] Cleaning products, routine, prohibited practices, and inspection intervals are written.
- [ ] Spare material is labelled with storage and shelf-life instructions, if supplied.
- [ ] Punch-list items and accepted appearance variations are recorded.
- [ ] Reopening authorization identifies permitted use and date/time.

Score each bid as **clear**, **assumption**, or **missing** beside every applicable item. Clarify assumptions before comparing price. The strongest proposal is not necessarily the longest; it is the one that turns important decisions into verifiable scope and leaves little room for two parties to imagine different floors.

*This checklist is general procurement guidance, not legal, engineering, insurance, or occupational-health advice. Verify requirements and documents with qualified Alberta professionals.*
