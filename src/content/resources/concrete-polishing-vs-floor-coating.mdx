The first question is not “Which lasts longer?” It is “Do you want the concrete itself to be the finished surface, or do you want a separate material system over it?” Polishing refines and exposes the slab. A resinous coating builds a film or system above the slab. That basic choice shapes appearance, repairs, moisture tolerance, maintenance, and future changes.

## The short comparison

| Decision factor | Polished concrete | Resinous floor coating |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Visual character | Reveals aggregate, cracks, patches, and slab variation | Can introduce opaque colour, flake, quartz, gloss, or texture |
| Relationship to slab | The slab becomes the finish | A bonded layer is added to the slab |
| Defect visibility | Repairs and historic marks remain part of the surface | Some variation can be visually reduced, but movement may still reflect |
| Chemical exposure | Depends on concrete, densifier/sealer, and maintenance | System can be selected for named exposures using product data |
| Texture | Developed through the grinding/polishing sequence and any treatment | Adjusted through aggregate, broadcast, film build, and topcoat |
| Future work | Regrinding or local repair affects appearance | Local recoating or patching may differ in colour and texture |
| Moisture | Still requires slab and treatment evaluation | Must meet the complete system’s moisture requirements |

This is not a universal winner table. It is a way to identify which questions deserve testing and samples.

## Choose polishing when the slab is the design

Polishing is a strong candidate when natural concrete variation is welcome, the slab is reasonably consistent, and the owner accepts that cracks, aggregate exposure, patches, and edge work will remain visible. ACI’s decorative-concrete guide covers variables such as materials, construction, finishing, protection, and maintenance that affect decorative results ([ACI PRC-310-19](https://www.concrete.org/store/productdetail.aspx?ItemID=31019)).

Before choosing a sheen from a photograph, investigate:

- aggregate exposure in representative test areas;
- previous floor coverings and adhesive shadows;
- hardness differences between placements or repairs;
- edge and corner access;
- crack and joint appearance;
- existing sealers, stains, and contamination;
- the maintenance method available to the owner.

The phrase “polished concrete” is incomplete without the intended aggregate exposure, final refinement level, treatment products, edge standard, mock-up location, and acceptance criteria.

## Choose a coating when the added layer has a job

A coating becomes more compelling when the owner needs an opaque field, deliberate texture, a system built around a named chemical exposure, easier visual zoning, or a broadcast finish. It may also provide a clearer path to visually unify dissimilar concrete—within the limitations of movement and substrate condition.

The coating route requires its own detailed choices: preparation method, target profile, repairs, primer, body coat, broadcast, topcoat, thickness, joints, and cure conditions. ICRI 310.2R explains that surface-preparation methods have different capabilities, operating requirements, and limitations, and provides profiles for specifying and verifying the prepared surface ([ICRI 310.2R](https://store.icri.org/item/3102r2013-english-selecting-concrete-surface-preparation-sealers-coatings-polymer-overlays-concrete-repair-342520)).

Do not select a coating solely because the existing slab looks poor. If the concrete is unsound or moisture conditions exceed the proposed system’s limits, an opaque finish does not remove those constraints.

## Five questions that decide more than gloss

### 1. What should repairs look like?

Both options inherit the slab’s cracks and patches. Polishing usually celebrates or at least reveals them. A coating may disguise colour variation, yet filled cracks can remain visible and moving cracks may reappear. ACI connects repair strategy to evaluation, preparation, application, and quality control ([ACI PRC-546-23](https://www.concrete.org/store/productdetail.aspx?ItemID=54623)). Ask for a mock-up containing a real repair, not only sound new concrete.

### 2. What contacts the floor?

List cleaners, oils, food acids, salts, solvents, hot equipment, tyres, rubber mats, and standing water. For a coating, request manufacturer test data relevant to the actual concentration, temperature, contact time, and cleaning routine. For polished concrete, request the same clarity about any densifier, guard, or sealer and its maintenance.

### 3. How will slip risk be managed?

“Polished” and “textured coating” are not complete slip-resistance specifications. Describe footwear, contamination, slopes, drains, cleaning, and transitions. Agree on a sample texture and a documented test or acceptance method suited to the project.

### 4. Who maintains it?

Ask the maintenance team which machines, pads, cleaners, and skills it already has. Polished concrete may rely on a defined dust-mopping, cleaning, and periodic refinement or guard routine. A coating may require non-damaging cleaners, protection from abrasive soil, and inspection for worn topcoat or local damage. The best finish is the one the facility can maintain as specified.

### 5. What downtime can the operation absorb?

Polishing involves repeated mechanical passes, edge work, cleaning, and treatments. Coating involves mechanical preparation, repairs, sequential layers, recoat windows, and cure gates. Compare full work plans, including equipment moves and reopening—not slogans such as “one day” or “no downtime.”

## Use two adjacent mock-ups

For a meaningful comparison, place polishing and coating mock-ups on representative concrete. Include a crack, an edge, and a repaired area where possible. Evaluate them under the actual lighting and from normal viewing distance. Record:

- aggregate exposure or coating colour and texture;
- visibility of repairs and joints;
- cleanability using the planned method;
- slip strategy when clean and under expected contamination;
- edge, drain, and transition details;
- acceptance limits for natural variation.

The decision should emerge from the room’s use and the slab’s evidence. If the natural concrete is desirable and its variation acceptable, polishing keeps that material at centre stage. If the floor needs a distinct functional layer or opaque design, a properly specified coating may fit better. In either case, the quality of evaluation, repairs, execution, and maintenance matters more than the category name.

*This comparison is not a project specification. Have qualified parties assess the slab, safety needs, exposures, and current manufacturer requirements before selection.*
