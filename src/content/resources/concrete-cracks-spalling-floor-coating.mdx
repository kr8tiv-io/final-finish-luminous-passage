Cracks and spalls are not one defect. A narrow dormant crack, a moving joint, a thin scaled surface, and a deep patch around corroding reinforcement need different responses. Covering every line with the same filler may improve a photograph while leaving the cause and movement unchanged.

For local scope options, see our [concrete preparation](/services/concrete-solutions).

ACI’s concrete-repair guide organizes repair work around evaluation, selection of repair strategy and materials, preparation, application, and quality control ([ACI PRC-546-23](https://www.concrete.org/store/productdetail.aspx?ItemID=54623)). Use that sequence before deciding what the finished floor should look like.

## Name the condition accurately

**Crack:** a separation in the concrete. Record width, length, location, moisture, edge level, and whether it changes.

**Control or contraction joint:** an intentional plane intended to manage where shrinkage cracking occurs. It is a floor detail, not automatically a defect.

**Construction joint:** where separate concrete placements meet. Its movement and loading conditions may differ from a random crack.

**Scaling:** loss of a thin surface layer, often seen as flaking or a rough shallow area.

**Spall:** a localized loss of concrete with measurable depth. Its edges may be weak or undercut, and the damaged area may extend beyond what is visually missing.

**Delamination:** a separation below the visible surface. The top may remain in place but sound hollow or break during preparation.

**Pop-out:** a small conical fragment that separates, often around a particle. Multiple pop-outs should be mapped rather than treated as isolated pinholes.

These labels do not establish a cause. They simply prevent unlike conditions from being priced as one bucket of “crack repair.”

## Use a triage path

### 1. Is there displacement or ongoing movement?

Compare the two sides of the crack with a straightedge. Look for offset, crushed edges, repeated repairs that reopened, and a line continuing through walls or structural elements. A coating contractor should not be expected to diagnose structural movement from appearance alone. Escalate significant displacement or suspected structural behaviour to an appropriately qualified professional.

### 2. Is water or contamination involved?

Record dampness, mineral deposits, oil, cleaning chemicals, and any history of leakage. A wet crack may be a pathway or merely a visible low point; it requires investigation before a dry repair product is selected.

### 3. Is the surrounding concrete sound?

The repair boundary should not be drawn only around the missing piece. Preparation can reveal weak edges or hidden delamination. ACI includes evaluation and removal of unsound concrete as part of the repair process, not as optional cosmetic work ([ACI PRC-546-23](https://www.concrete.org/store/productdetail.aspx?ItemID=54623)).

### 4. Must the line remain a joint?

Ask how intentional joints will be treated. Filling a moving joint rigidly, bridging it, or carrying it through the coating are different design decisions with different appearance and maintenance consequences. The proposal should name the chosen detail.

## Match the intervention to the observation

| Observed condition | Information needed before repair | Coating-plan implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fine, level crack | Movement history, moisture, contamination | May remain visible or reflect; define treatment and expectation |
| Reopened previous fill | Prior material, movement, edge condition | Remove failed material and reassess cause |
| Offset or widening crack | Structural context and monitoring | Obtain qualified evaluation before cosmetic work |
| Shallow scaling | Depth and soundness after preparation | Remove weak surface; verify remaining substrate |
| Deep spall | Extent, cause, reinforcement, repair depth | Select repair method and cure/overcoat sequence |
| Hollow area | Delamination boundary | Remove or evaluate unsound section before coating |
| Wet crack | Water source and moisture condition | Resolve water issue and check system compatibility |

No table can select a repair product from a photograph. Repair materials differ in thickness range, substrate-conditioning requirements, cure, movement capacity, and compatibility with later layers. Capture those requirements from the current manufacturer documents.

## What good preparation looks like

Preparation must create sound boundaries, remove contaminants and weak material, and leave the surface required by the repair or coating. ICRI 310.2R summarizes the capabilities, operating requirements, and limitations of concrete preparation methods and provides benchmark profiles for specification and verification ([ICRI 310.2R](https://store.icri.org/item/3102r2013-english-selecting-concrete-surface-preparation-sealers-coatings-polymer-overlays-concrete-repair-342520)).

That means “grind and fill cracks” is too vague. The scope should say:

- which cracks, joints, spalls, and weak areas are included;
- how repair boundaries will be established;
- how dust and debris will be removed;
- the repair product and permitted placement range;
- required concrete and ambient conditions;
- cure or overcoat criteria before the next layer;
- how repaired areas will be checked;
- what unit rate or approval process applies to concealed damage.

## Set appearance expectations before coating

Even a technically appropriate repair can telegraph as a colour, texture, or level variation. A thin finish shows substrate irregularity differently from a thicker broadcast system. Movement can reappear. Patching every visible mark does not make an old slab visually equivalent to new concrete.

Decide which outcome matters most: preserving functional joints, reducing trip edges, producing a uniform decorative field, resisting a known exposure, or making future repairs accessible. Request a sample or mock-up when appearance at repairs is important, and document which defects are accepted rather than repaired.

The responsible next step is a marked floor plan with each condition classified and a written repair allowance for what preparation may reveal. That turns hidden deterioration into a managed project risk instead of an argument after the surface is opened.

*This guide cannot diagnose structural movement, corrosion, or water entry. Seek qualified evaluation for displaced cracks, exposed reinforcement, widespread delamination, or recurring moisture, and follow current repair-product instructions.*
