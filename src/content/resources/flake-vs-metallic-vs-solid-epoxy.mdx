## The quick comparison

- **Flake** is the practical choice for most garages. A base coat, a broadcast of coloured flake, a scrape, and a clear top coat make a textured, speckled floor that hides dust, grit, and small scratches.
- **Solid colour** is one even colour under a top coat. It looks clean and architectural and brightens a room, but it shows dust, tire marks, and scratches sooner.
- **Metallic** is poured and worked by hand so metallic pigments settle into swirls and depth. No two floors are alike. It is the most decorative of the three and the least forgiving of dust and wear.

All three are epoxy systems on prepared concrete. The preparation underneath follows the same idea for each, because the prepared surface has to suit the coating, as the [ICRI guideline on surface preparation](https://store.icri.org/item/3102r2013-english-selecting-concrete-surface-preparation-sealers-coatings-polymer-overlays-concrete-repair-342520) sets out. What changes is what you see, and how the floor lives with daily use.

## How each one looks

Flake reads as texture from across the room and as colour up close. The colour comes from the blend, a mix of tones and chip sizes. Full coverage, where flake covers the whole base, gives a granite-like surface. Partial coverage lets the base colour show between the chips. Our [flake colour guide](/resources/floor-flake-colour-design-guide/) covers how blends read in different light.

Solid colour is calm. Greys, charcoals, and light neutrals are common in garages and workshops. A light solid floor bounces light around and makes a room feel bigger.

Metallic floors have movement. Light travels through the pigments, so the floor looks different at noon and at night. That depth is the appeal, and the variation is part of it: the final pattern is shaped by the pour and cannot be predicted exactly. Read the [metallic expectations guide](/resources/metallic-epoxy-expectations/) before choosing one.

## What each one hides, and what it shows

This is where most people decide.

- **Dust and grit.** Flake hides them best. Solid and metallic floors show a film of dust sooner, especially in dark colours.
- **Scratches.** Every coated floor scratches under grit and dragged equipment. Flake disguises fine scratches in its pattern. Solid and metallic floors show them in raking light.
- **Tire marks.** Hot tires can mark any coating, and the marks are easiest to see on light solid colours. The [hot-tire pickup guide](/resources/hot-tire-pickup-epoxy/) explains the causes.
- **Salt rings.** Edmonton winters bring meltwater and road salt into garages. The white rings it leaves when it dries show on dark solid and metallic floors. A mid-tone flake blend hides them.

## Grip underfoot

A smooth, glossy floor can be slippery when wet, whatever its colour. Flake adds a little texture on its own. For any of the three, an anti-slip top coat adds grip where people walk in wet boots or where water sits.

The [CCOHS guidance on preventing slips, trips, and falls](https://www.ccohs.ca/oshanswers/safety_haz/falls.html) is written for workplaces, and it names the quality of the walking surface as a key factor. The same holds in a garage or a mudroom. Our [slip resistance guide](/resources/slip-resistance-floor-coatings/) goes into texture and top coats.

## Which finish suits which room

- **Garage.** Flake, usually. It handles grit, salt, and daily parking, and still looks good between cleanings.
- **Basement living space.** Metallic or solid, where the floor is seen more and dragged on less. See the [basement guide](/resources/basement-concrete-floor-coating/).
- **Workshop.** A light solid colour for brightness, or flake for forgiveness. Grip where oil or water is around.
- **Showroom or commercial space.** Solid colour for a clean, even look, or metallic for a feature area. The [solid-colour commercial guide](/resources/solid-colour-commercial-flooring/) covers commercial choices.
- **Mudroom.** Flake with grip, because boots and water arrive together.

## Time on the floor, and what it means for the quote

The preparation underneath is the same idea for all three, so the differences come from the layers on top.

- **Flake** has a rhythm: base coat, broadcast, scrape, top coat. The broadcast is fast, and the scrape and top coat make the surface even.
- **Solid colour** goes down in rolled or squeegeed coats. It is the simplest to install, and the least forgiving of a poorly prepared slab, because every flaw in the concrete shows through an even colour.
- **Metallic** is poured and worked by hand while it is wet, so it takes the most skilled time on the floor. The look depends on that hand work, which is why two metallic floors never match.

Those differences show up in time and in the quote. Ask for the options side by side so you compare like with like; the [guide to garage coating costs](/resources/garage-floor-coating-cost-edmonton/) explains the other factors.

## Colour and light in an Edmonton garage

Garage light changes the colour you chose. Winter daylight is low and blue, and most garage lighting is cool LED. Warm greys can look flat under cool light, and very dark floors swallow light in a single-bulb garage. Before choosing, look at colours in your own space at the time of day you use it most. A light or mid-tone floor makes a garage brighter to work in, and hides salt rings better than black.

## Living with each one

All three clean the same way: a dust mop or soft broom, then a damp mop with a mild cleaner. The difference is how often you notice the need. Flake forgives a busy week. Solid and metallic floors look their best with more frequent dust mopping. The [cleaning guide](/resources/how-to-clean-coated-garage-floor/) has the details.
