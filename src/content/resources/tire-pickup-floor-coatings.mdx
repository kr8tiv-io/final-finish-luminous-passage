Tire pickup describes coating that softens, lifts, tears, or transfers at the tire contact area. It is different from a rubber mark sitting on top of an intact finish. Before choosing a cleaner, repair, or replacement, establish which condition is present and what the floor system actually is. This guide covers prevention and causes across garage floor coating systems generally; if tire damage has already appeared on a specific epoxy floor, the [hot-tire pickup diagnosis guide](/resources/hot-tire-pickup-epoxy/) walks through the incident record and repair evaluation for that situation.

For local scope options, see our [residential epoxy flooring](/services/residential-epoxy-flooring).

## Read the pattern like evidence

Photograph each parking position before cleaning. Mark front and rear tires, vehicle type, normal arrival time, and whether damage occurs after long drives. Record washing, heated tires, winter de-icer, oil, plasticizers from mats, and previous touch-ups. Note whether the edge is bonded, curled, soft, or exposing concrete.

A black transfer mark that cleans off with a manufacturer-permitted method is not proof of coating failure. Conversely, repeated lifting at tire footprints deserves more than another spot of paint. Preserve loose pieces and product records for assessment.

## Prevention begins below the finish

Concrete must be sound, clean, and prepared for the complete specified system. Oil, weak laitance, an unknown sealer, moisture conditions, or inadequate profile can undermine adhesion independently of tire heat. The [ACI concrete repair guide](https://www.concrete.org/store/productdetail.aspx?ItemID=54623) covers evaluation, surface preparation, repair, protective systems, and quality control. That framework supports connecting a failure to substrate evidence rather than diagnosing it from the word “epoxy.”

Ask the proposal to name the preparation method, edge work, repair materials, inspection point, and target surface condition. If oil staining is present, use the [oil-contamination planning guide](/resources/remove-oil-stains-before-floor-coating/) before mechanical preparation. Cleaning and profiling solve different problems.

## Product family is not a specification

Different primers, body coats, broadcasts, and topcoats have different written requirements. Ask the manufacturer or installer for current product data sheets covering approved substrates, preparation, environmental limits, mixing, layer thickness, recoat timing, cure stages, chemical exposure, cleaning, and vehicle return. Make sure all layers are intended to work together.

Avoid claims such as “hot-tire proof” unless the exact meaning, conditions, evidence, and limits are documented and approved for publication. A sample panel cannot reproduce every slab, vehicle, tire, parking pattern, or cure condition.

## Moisture is its own checkpoint

A dry surface or successful blasting does not establish internal slab moisture. [ASTM F2170](https://www.astm.org/f2170-19a.html) describes in-situ relative-humidity testing in concrete floor slabs. If the selected system requires that method, the scope should identify test locations, timing, reporting, and the product-specific limit. A moisture result informs selection; it does not guarantee performance under tires.

## Cure needs staged language

“Dry” can refer to touch, foot traffic, light loads, chemical exposure, or full service. The contractor should provide written restrictions for walking, storage, washing, vehicle parking, mats, and normal use. Conditions in a garage near an Edmonton overhead door can differ from those at the centre of the slab, so record air and substrate conditions as the manufacturer requires.

Do not rush vehicles back based on a generic number from another product. Keep the floor clean and protected during the cure period. If a vehicle must enter sooner than permitted, that is a scheduling conflict to resolve before application, not after.

## If damage already exists

Stop parking on soft or lifting zones where practical. Do not pull loose edges to “see how far it goes.” Photograph, clean only as permitted, and ask for an assessment that maps bonded and unbonded areas. A local repair may remain visible and is reasonable only when the cause, boundary, preparation, and compatibility are addressed.

Widespread failure, unknown materials, repeated patches, dampness, or contamination may point to broader removal. The [repair-or-replace assessment](/resources/repair-or-replace-coated-floor/) explains how to compare local repair, recoat, and replacement scopes.

## Questions for a garage-coating quote

1. What observed conditions create tire-pickup risk here?
2. Which exact layers are proposed, and where are their current data sheets?
3. How will oil, old coatings, sealers, repairs, and edges be handled?
4. What preparation acceptance check occurs before primer?
5. What moisture information does this system require?
6. What environmental records are kept during application and cure?
7. When are foot traffic, washing, mats, and vehicles permitted?
8. How would a tire-contact repair be evaluated and blended?

Tire pickup is not prevented by a resin label alone. It is managed through condition assessment, substrate preparation, compatible layers, controlled application, adequate cure, and responsible use. ## Add seasonal maintenance to the plan

Vehicle areas need routine observation after handover. Sweep or vacuum grit before wet cleaning, remove de-icer residue with a manufacturer-permitted method, and contain new leaks promptly. Check tire positions, door thresholds, drain edges, cracks, and repair boundaries during cleaning. Avoid sharp metal tools, unapproved solvents, and aggressive pads.

Keep mats or trays only when both their manufacturer and the coating documentation support the intended contact. Lift them periodically when permitted to inspect for trapped moisture, residue, or marking. Do not leave an unknown rubber or plastic backing on a new finish simply because it is sold as garage protection.

Create a small log with photographs at handover, after the first winter, and whenever the vehicle or use changes. Record cleaners, tools, spills, and unusual marks. Early evidence helps distinguish a removable transfer from developing adhesion loss. It also gives a contractor useful context before a repair spreads beyond the original tire position.

If parking changes to a heavier vehicle, different tire type, frequent long-distance arrival, or equipment with concentrated wheels, disclose that change and request a new product-specific review. The installed system was selected for documented conditions, not every future garage use.
