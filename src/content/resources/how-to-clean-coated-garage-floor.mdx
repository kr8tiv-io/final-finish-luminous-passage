## The reliable routine: dry soil first, wet cleaning second

Loose grit behaves like an abrasive under shoes, tires, rolling cabinets, and snow-removal tools. Begin by removing it with a soft broom, dust mop, or vacuum tool approved for the finish. Work from the back of the garage toward the door and pay attention to flake texture, edges, corners, parking tracks, and drains.

For local scope options, see our [residential epoxy flooring](/services/residential-epoxy-flooring).

Only then move to wet cleaning. Start with the coating manufacturer's maintenance instructions, including cleaner type, dilution, water temperature, dwell time, agitation tool, rinsing, and drying. If those instructions are missing, request them before experimenting. “Epoxy-safe” on a retail label does not prove compatibility with the actual topcoat.

## A practical wash sequence

1. Move vehicles and portable belongings; protect anything that cannot get wet.
2. Photograph unusual stains, loose edges, blisters, or cracks before cleaning.
3. Sweep or vacuum thoroughly.
4. Pre-treat only with a product and concentration accepted by the coating supplier.
5. Apply the maintenance solution in manageable sections.
6. Agitate with the approved soft brush or pad without letting dirty solution dry.
7. Pick up the solution or direct it only where drainage and local rules allow.
8. Rinse if the product instructions require it, then remove standing water.
9. Let the floor dry and inspect from several angles.

Change dirty water rather than spreading it. Cleaner residue can leave haze or create a different walking feel, so rinsing and recovery matter where specified.

## Edmonton winter soil needs seasonal attention

Meltwater can carry grit and de-icing residue into parking tracks and the doorway band. Use trays or mats only if their backing and placement are compatible with the coating, and lift them periodically to inspect and clean beneath them.

The [Alberta Infrastructure de-icing concrete bulletin](https://www.alberta.ca/system/files/custom_downloaded_images/tr-tb-001a.pdf) discusses salt scaling, saturation, and freeze-thaw exposure and recommends thoroughly washing concrete surfaces exposed to de-icers each spring. A coated residential garage is not the bulletin's infrastructure application, but the maintenance cue is useful: do not allow accumulated winter residue to become the default condition. Follow the coating maker's wash method and manage rinse water appropriately.

## Match the response to the spill

For fresh oil or automotive fluid, contain and lift the material promptly with methods allowed by the maintenance guide. Avoid spreading it across a larger area. Consult both the fluid label or safety data sheet and coating instructions before choosing a cleaner. Ventilate as directed and keep ignition sources and incompatible chemicals away.

For rubber marks or unidentified dark streaks, begin with the mildest approved method. A mark on top is not the same as coating softened or pulled from the slab. If film is lifting beneath a tire, stop cleaning and use the [hot-tire pickup documentation guide](/resources/hot-tire-pickup-epoxy/).

For paint, adhesive, rust-coloured deposits, or construction residue, do not reach immediately for solvent, a razor, or an abrasive pad. Ask the coating supplier for a compatible method. Scraping can cut the finish, while solvents can change gloss or soften some materials.

## Tools and products to question before use

Confirm compatibility before using concentrated degreasers, citrus cleaners, bleach mixtures, acids, solvents, powdered abrasives, steam, pressure washers, stiff wire brushes, or aggressive machine pads. Never mix cleaning chemicals. Product labels, safety data sheets, ventilation requirements, and disposal instructions still apply even when a cleaner is accepted for the floor.

If a pressure washer or autoscrubber is proposed, request limits for pressure, nozzle, distance, pad, brush, and water recovery. Edges, joints, damaged areas, and transitions may need gentler treatment. Test an unfamiliar approved method in an inconspicuous area and let it dry before judging gloss or colour.

## Inspect while you clean

Cleaning is the best time to note changes. Keep a dated photograph if you find a chip, crack, blister, cloudy area, worn texture, or separation at an edge. Mark its location on a simple garage sketch and compare it at the next cleaning.

Do not coat over, grind, or aggressively probe a questionable area before the installer sees it, especially if warranty notice may apply. The [ACI concrete-repair guide](https://www.concrete.org/store/productdetail.aspx?ItemID=54623) treats condition assessment, preparation, repair, and quality control as connected work—a useful reason to document before changing the evidence. The [floor-coating warranty guide](/resources/floor-coating-warranty-questions/) lists records and claim-process questions to preserve.

## Build a maintenance card

Keep a one-page card with the system and topcoat name, approved routine cleaner and dilution, allowed tools, prohibited products, spill contact, and installer details. Store the current manufacturer instructions with project documents. This prevents a future owner, cleaner, or employee from guessing.

## Diagnose haze before cleaning again

If a dry floor looks cloudy, compare an untouched area, a rinsed test area, and the normal walking path. Haze may be residue, fine scratching, moisture beneath a temporary covering, or a change in the coating. Repeating stronger cleaning without knowing which can make the evidence harder to read.

Photograph the condition in side light and ask the installer which approved test distinguishes residue from film change. If the maintenance method is responsible, update the cleaning card so another person does not repeat it. If the coating appears soft, loose, or blistered, restrict the area and request an assessment.

Date the maintenance card whenever an instruction changes, the floor is recoated, or a cleaner is approved. Frequency should follow actual soil rather than a universal calendar. Sweep when grit is present, address spills promptly, and schedule fuller washing around winter accumulation and workspace use.
