Concrete surface profile, usually shortened to CSP, describes the roughness and texture left after concrete preparation. Imagine the difference between a smooth countertop, coarse sandpaper, and exposed stone: each presents a different shape for the next material to contact. CSP gives project teams a shared visual reference for that shape.

For local scope options, see our [concrete preparation](/services/concrete-solutions).

It does **not** certify that the concrete is clean, dry, strong, or ready to coat. Those are separate acceptance questions.

## What the CSP numbers mean

ICRI Guideline 310.2R includes benchmark profiles and discusses the capabilities, operating requirements, and limitations of preparation methods used before sealers, coatings, overlays, and repairs ([ICRI 310.2R](https://store.icri.org/item/3102r2013-english-selecting-concrete-surface-preparation-sealers-coatings-polymer-overlays-concrete-repair-342520)). The ICRI system runs from CSP 1 through CSP 10, with the visual texture becoming progressively more aggressive.

The number is a comparator, not a recipe. “Diamond grind” and “shot blast” are methods; “CSP 2” or “CSP 4” is an intended surface result. Tool type, media, speed, pressure, concrete hardness, existing finish, operator technique, and repeated passes can change what a method produces.

## Where the target comes from

The target profile should come from the current instructions or specification for the actual material being bonded. A thin sealer, high-build coating, polymer overlay, and repair mortar do not necessarily call for the same texture. The specifier reconciles the material requirement with the existing slab and project constraints.

A useful scope says:

> Mechanically prepare sound concrete to the profile required by Product X’s current data sheet, provisionally CSP __, then verify against ICRI comparator chips and the project acceptance procedure.

A weak scope says:

> Grind floor as needed.

The first statement defines the material, reference, provisional target, and verification. The second defines only an activity.

## How comparison works in the field

ICRI’s official R3 guide library lists a field reference for using CSP replica chips ([ICRI R3 Concrete Guides](https://www.icri.org/resources/icri-technical-publications/r3-concrete-guides/)). The replica chips are tactile and visual comparators. The prepared concrete is compared with the benchmark texture under suitable light and across representative areas.

The inspection should include more than the open centre of a room:

- edges and corners where a different tool was used;
- high and low areas;
- dense or soft placements;
- repaired sections;
- locations where old coating or adhesive was removed;
- ramps, drains, coves, and vertical returns;
- transitions between equipment passes.

Photographs are helpful records but can flatten texture and change scale. They should supplement, not replace, the specified on-site comparison.

## CSP is only one line on the acceptance sheet

| Acceptance question | Why CSP does not answer it | Separate check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Is the concrete sound? | Weak material can still look rough | Sounding, visual evaluation, or specified bond testing |
| Is it clean? | Oil and dust can remain in a textured surface | Inspection and specified cleaning verification |
| Is it dry enough? | Roughness does not measure internal moisture | Manufacturer-required moisture test |
| Is laitance removed? | A rough-looking weak layer may remain locally | Inspect and test the prepared substrate |
| Is the profile uniform? | One acceptable spot does not represent the room | Grid or representative-area inspection |
| Is the surface too rough? | More texture is not always better for a thin layer | Compare with the product range and film build |

ACI’s repair guide likewise frames surface preparation within a broader process of concrete evaluation, material selection, application, and quality control ([ACI PRC-546-23](https://www.concrete.org/store/productdetail.aspx?ItemID=54623)).

## Why “rougher is better” fails

An unnecessarily aggressive profile can remove sound concrete, expose aggregate, increase repair or material consumption, create high points that show through a thin coating, and complicate edge matching. An insufficient profile can leave a smooth or contaminated surface that does not meet the bonded material’s requirement.

The correct target is therefore a range tied to the selected system. If the current data sheet is silent, request written direction from the manufacturer or qualified specifier instead of guessing upward.

## Turn CSP into an inspection hold point

The most useful time to inspect profile is after preparation and cleanup but before primer conceals the surface. Define a hold point in the schedule:

1. Contractor completes the representative prepared area.
2. Dust and debris are removed using the specified method.
3. Project parties compare the surface with the specified reference.
4. Cleanliness, soundness, defects, repairs, and moisture documentation are reviewed separately.
5. Any adjustment is agreed before the full area proceeds or before primer application.

For a large or variable slab, establish a benchmark area at the beginning and protect it as a comparison reference. Record the tool setup only as supporting information; accept the resulting surface, not the machine name.

## Questions to put in a proposal

- Which layer controls the target CSP?
- What current manufacturer document states the requirement?
- Which preparation method is proposed, and what are its limitations here?
- How will edges, repairs, and difficult access match the field area?
- Who supplies the comparator chips or other specified verification tool?
- When is the inspection hold point?
- What other tests establish cleanliness, soundness, and moisture suitability?
- What happens if the prepared surface is outside the target?

CSP becomes valuable when it connects a material requirement to a field-verifiable result. Used alone, it is just a texture number. Used with condition assessment, cleaning, moisture evaluation, repairs, and documented acceptance, it helps make “properly prepared” something the owner and installer can actually discuss.

*This overview does not reproduce the ICRI guideline or set a profile for any product. Use the current manufacturer requirement, project specification, and qualified field verification.*
