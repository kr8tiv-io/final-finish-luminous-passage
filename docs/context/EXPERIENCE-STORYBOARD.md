## 1. Thesis

The visitor watches one material become a floor, and then follows that floor everywhere it goes. First they see it liquid, poured and levelling under their own scroll. Then, on white, they hear thirty people say it was done right. Then they are taken beneath it: a slow walk up a stair, the floor peeling open into the layers that were ground, filled, broadcast, scraped, and coated before anyone saw a colour. Then the floor walks into rooms, three of them, side by side, each one folding the last off the screen. Then the concept ends and the evidence begins: real Edmonton concrete on one side of a divider and the finished floor on the other, a rotating passage of them, and then all ninety-six photographs at once, flown over and resolved into a single job. After a breath of white the same material comes back as a choice, revealed through its own silhouette, then scaled up to a working showroom, then set aside so Denis can ask four plain questions. The visitor learns, in order: what this is, that it is trusted, that the work is underneath, what it makes possible, that it is real, that it is theirs to choose, that it scales, and how to start. The single connecting subject is **the floor material itself** — one persistent WebGL floor plane (`SurfaceFloor`) whose material state morphs across every act (liquid pearl, ground concrete, cured pearl, flake, photograph, metallic, satin, paper) and whose camera is always at standing or kneeling height, so the page never stops being about the thing under your feet.

## 4. Continuity

**What persists.** One `<canvas>` (`SurfaceCanvas`, `transition:persist`), one header (`transition:persist`, progress line, per-act theme, chapter dots at ≥ 1200 px, "disappearing chrome" during pinned interiors, returns on pause or pointer), one paper ground, and one floor-material state (`uMaterial` 0–7) that only ever changes by `material-morph`. The 3D menu opens over the live floor plane; hovering a destination blends the floor toward that act's material with `quickTo`, so the menu is a preview of where you are going, not a list.

**Hand-offs — never a cut.** Preloader → 1 dissolve; 1 → S1 `resin-wipe`; S1 → 2 `settle` then `line-rise`; 2 → 3 `preparation-curl`; 3 → 4 `swallow`; 4 interior `fold-and-shoot-left` + `resin-swirl-plate`; 4 → 5 `shrink-to-card` with `horizontal-pull`; 5 → 6 `unwrap-to-lattice`; 6 → S2 `white-dissolve`; S2 → 7 `resin-layer-mask`; 7 → 8 `aperture`; 8 → 9 `white-dissolve`; 9 → 10 `fold`; 10 → footer `settle`. Every boundary passes the Step 8 boundary spec (`p ∈ {.05,.25,.5,.75,.95}` forward and reverse, `pixelmatch(p=.49,p=.51) < 2 %`), and in `balanced` the DOM crossfade **is** the transition.

**The two silences.** S1 after the pour (one line, floor at rest) and S2 after the archive resolves (wordless). They exist so the loudest moments — the fold-and-shoot-left train and the lattice flyover — have something quiet to land on. Neither is an empty spacer: the horizon line is on screen in both, and the material state is blending underneath.
