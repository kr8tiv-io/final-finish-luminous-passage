Concrete Moisture Testing Guide

Use concrete moisture testing before Edmonton floor coatings to match methods, locations, and limits to the slab rather than one casual meter reading.

Updated
September 8, 2026

Ask Denis about your floor

A concrete moisture test is useful only when three things are named: the method, the installation conditions, and the coating system’s acceptance limit. A meter swept across the surface may help an assessor compare locations, but it does not become an ASTM test merely because it displays a number.

For local scope options, see our concrete preparation.

Moisture testing also does not answer every water question. It does not locate a plumbing leak, prove exterior drainage is sound, or guarantee how a slab will behave forever.

First decide what you are trying to learn

Before testing, write the decision the result will inform:

  • Can the specified primer or coating be installed under the manufacturer’s published moisture limit?
  • Are readings consistent across the proposed work area?
  • Has a recently uncovered or repaired area reached the condition required for the next step?
  • Does an unexpected wet location require water-source investigation?
  • Is a mitigation option needed, and what evidence supports it?

ASTM F2170 states that moisture permeating from concrete can affect flooring and coatings, and that internal relative-humidity testing is one method manufacturers may require before installation (ASTM F2170-19a). It also states an essential limitation: results indicate the slab condition at the time of the test.

Do not mix unlike methods

Method What the official method addresses What to request in the report
In-situ relative humidity Quantitative percent relative humidity within the slab under ASTM F2170 Probe locations, slab details, equilibration and readings required by the edition used
Calcium-chloride test Moisture vapour emission from a prepared concrete surface under ASTM F1869 Test area preparation, exposure period, mass data, calculated result and locations
Handheld electrical meter Instrument-specific comparative or screening reading Model, mode, calibration/check procedure, locations, and stated limitation
Plastic-sheet observation Qualitative observation under a specified procedure Procedure, duration, conditions, observations; not a numeric substitute

ASTM F1869 is a distinct standard for measuring moisture vapour emission rate from a concrete subfloor using anhydrous calcium chloride (ASTM F1869-23). Its result and units cannot be renamed “relative humidity,” just as an in-situ RH result cannot be reported as an emission rate.

The product manufacturer may require one method, several methods, or additional conditions. Use the current documents for the exact primer and complete floor system.

Build a defensible test plan

A test plan should exist before holes are drilled or kits are opened. Include:

  1. Drawing: outline the work area, slab boundaries if known, exterior edges, drains, previous flooring, repairs, and suspicious locations.
  2. Method and edition: cite the complete standard designation rather than “moisture test.”
  3. Tester and equipment: identify who performs the work and how equipment conformity or calibration is documented.
  4. Quantity and distribution: follow the test method and project specification; add justified locations for wet observations or separate slab areas.
  5. Building conditions: document whether the space is operating at expected service conditions and whether old flooring was recently removed.
  6. Acceptance criteria: quote the system manufacturer’s current limit and any required preparation or mitigation.
  7. Decision path: say what happens after a pass, a fail, a mixed set, or an invalid test.

Without an acceptance criterion, testing produces data but no project decision. Without a location map, a low result from one corner can be incorrectly applied to the entire floor.

Read the report critically

Look for raw readings as well as a pass/fail label. Ask:

  • Do probe or kit locations match the floor plan?
  • Are the units correct for the method?
  • Were required time and environmental conditions recorded?
  • Is any result missing, damaged, or identified as invalid?
  • Do results cluster near doors, exterior walls, drains, or former floor coverings?
  • Is the quoted acceptance limit traceable to the exact proposed product and current data sheet?
  • Does the report separate observation from interpretation?

Do not average a high location out of existence. A mixed result may indicate different slab placements, exposure history, coverings, vapour-retarder conditions, or a localized water source. It may justify more testing or investigation rather than immediate coating.

Connect moisture to the rest of the substrate

Moisture is one input. The slab must also be sound, clean, appropriately profiled, and repaired. ACI’s concrete-repair guide connects condition evaluation, material selection, surface preparation, and quality control (ACI PRC-546-23). A passing moisture result does not override oil contamination, weak laitance, moving cracks, or an incompatible repair.

Likewise, a result above a standard system’s published limit does not prove the only answer is a “moisture barrier.” Ask for the named mitigation system, its own substrate requirements and limits, the test evidence supporting its use, and how joints or water entry are treated. Compare postponement, source correction, retesting, and a manufacturer-approved alternative.

The minimum documentation to keep

Retain the test plan, floor map, raw results, photographs, equipment identifiers, applicable product data sheets, and the final decision. Record which areas were covered by the conclusion and which remained untested. If construction conditions or the selected system change, reassess whether the old results still answer the new question.

The practical standard for a proposal is simple: another informed person should be able to trace the test location, method, reading, product limit, and resulting action without relying on memory.

Moisture standards contain detailed procedures and safety provisions that this overview does not reproduce. Testing, drilling, water-source diagnosis, and coating selection should be performed by competent parties using current standards and manufacturer instructions.
