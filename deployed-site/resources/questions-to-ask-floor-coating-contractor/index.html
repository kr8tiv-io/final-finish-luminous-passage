Ask Your Floor Coating Contractor

Ask your Edmonton floor coating contractor about diamond grinding, flake systems, cure timing, garage access, and documentation so quotes stay comparable.

Updated
September 14, 2026

Ask Denis about your floor

Ask about the floor before asking about the brand

A useful contractor conversation starts with observations. What did they see in the concrete, what remains unknown, and how does that evidence shape preparation and system choice? Product names matter later, when they are tied to a written layer sequence and current technical documents.

For local scope options, see our residential epoxy flooring.

Use the questions below during the site visit. Ask for important answers in the proposal rather than relying on memory.

Site condition and measurement

  1. Which areas did you inspect, and which were hidden or inaccessible? Stored goods, mats, cabinets, or old flooring can conceal conditions.
  2. What cracks, spalls, patches, joints, contamination, old coatings, or moisture indicators did you observe? Request photographs or a marked sketch.
  3. How was the project area measured? Confirm floors, steps, curbs, vertical surfaces, and excluded areas.
  4. Which building or drainage problems are outside the coating scope? A coating should not be presented as a structural or water-entry repair without evidence.

Preparation and repairs

  1. How will oil, de-icing residue, old coating, sealer, adhesive, and loose concrete be treated? Cleaning and mechanical profiling solve different problems.
  2. Which preparation method and required surface condition apply to the selected system? Ask about edges, drains, posts, steps, and dust collection.
  3. How will each crack, joint, and spall be handled? Confirm whether movement can reappear and whether repairs may remain visible.
  4. What happens if preparation exposes more damage? The change process should name the decision-maker, documentation, price method, and pause point.

The garage preparation guide shows the detail a preparation answer should contain.

Moisture and product selection

  1. Is moisture investigation warranted here, and why? Ask for test method, locations, timing, results, and the exact product limits.
  2. What is every layer in the proposed system? List repair material, primer, body coat, broadcast, intermediate coat, and topcoat by manufacturer and product.
  3. Who confirms compatibility? This is especially important when products come from different manufacturers.
  4. Which current technical data sheets govern preparation, mixing, site conditions, film build, recoat, and service? Keep copies with the contract.

Avoid accepting category claims such as “epoxy lasts longer” or “polyaspartic is always better.” Formulations and complete systems vary.

Appearance and real use

  1. What exactly does the sample approve? Record base colour, blend, broadcast, texture, sheen, and acceptable variation.
  2. How will the floor meet edges, drains, doors, stairs, walls, and adjacent finishes? Transition details prevent later surprises.
  3. How did you account for tires, grit, sunlight, water, cleaners, rolling loads, impact, or chemicals? Ask for limitations as well as benefits.
  4. How is traction being discussed? No floor is universally slip-proof; contaminant, footwear, slope, texture, wear, and cleaning all matter.

Crew, workplace, and site controls

  1. Who performs and supervises the work? Ask which tasks may be subcontracted and who is your daily contact.
  2. How are dust, noise, ventilation, power, waste, security, and access handled? Include pets and household entry for homes; exits, occupants, and other trades for businesses.
  3. Which safety data sheets apply, and who reviews building-specific controls? For workplaces, Alberta provides the official OHS Act, Regulation and Code. The employer and work-site parties must determine applicable duties; a generic proposal is not the site safety plan.
  4. How does air move to occupied areas? The Canadian Centre for Occupational Health and Safety explains that indoor air quality concerns can involve sources, ventilation, and occupant information. Qualified site personnel should translate that framework into controls for the actual building.

Schedule and return to use

  1. What conditions could change the schedule? Slab temperature, ambient conditions, repairs, access, and recoat windows may matter.
  2. When can each activity resume? Ask separately about inspection, walking, furniture, mats, cleaning, vehicles, rolling loads, and chemicals.
  3. What event starts each waiting period, and where is it documented? A finish feeling dry is not a universal clearance.
  4. Who communicates and approves a delay? A realistic contingency is more valuable than an unsupported exact promise.

Read how epoxy coatings cure for the reason these milestones should remain separate.

Price, handover, and warranty

  1. What is included, excluded, allowed for, or unknown? Compare scope before totals.
  2. How are taxes, mobilization, storage, furniture moving, repairs, edges, cleanup, and disposal shown? Avoid filling gaps with assumptions.
  3. What records are delivered? Request system and colour details, product documents, application or condition records promised in the scope, photographs, and maintenance instructions.
  4. What does the warranty actually cover? Separate installer workmanship, manufacturer terms, concrete conditions, exclusions, maintenance, notice, inspection, and remedy.
  5. How should a scratch, stain, chip, or localized failure be reported and repaired? Ask about visual limitations.
  6. Which company details can I verify? Confirm legal name, contact details, insurance or credentials relevant to the job, and references directly.

Use the proposal comparison worksheet to place bidders side by side. A careful contractor may answer some questions with “we need testing” or “we will know after preparation.” That is more useful than false certainty when the unknown and decision process are documented.
