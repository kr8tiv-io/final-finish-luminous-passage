Garage Coating Costs in Edmonton

What sets the price of a garage floor coating in Edmonton: floor size, slab condition, grinding, repairs, the coating system, the finish, and the schedule.

Updated
September 22, 2026

Ask Denis about your floor

The short answer

There is no honest price per square foot for a garage floor until someone has seen the concrete. The price of a coated garage floor in Edmonton comes from seven things. They are the size and shape of the floor, the condition of the slab, the grinding it needs, the repairs it needs, the coating system, the finish, and the schedule. Two garages with the same footprint can land far apart because the concrete under them is different.

Here is how each of those moves the number, and how to line up two quotes so they can be compared fairly.

Size is the start, not the answer

Square footage sets the amount of material and the time on the floor, so every quote starts there. It is not the whole story. Edges, corners, steps, curbs, posts, and floor drains all take hand tools and slower work. A long single garage can carry more edge per square metre than an open double. A floor with a step into the house, a drain, and a curb along two walls has more detail than its area suggests.

Measure the floor wall to wall, and note anything that breaks it up. It helps more than a rounded guess.

The slab decides the preparation

Preparation is the part of the job you never see again, and it is where most of the variation lives. A garage that has been parked in through Edmonton winters carries salt residue, grit, and oil where the vehicles sit. An older slab may have paint, a sealer, or patches from past repairs. Each of those has to come off, or be dealt with, before a coating can bond.

Diamond grinding opens the surface so the coating has something to hold. The ICRI guideline on surface preparation describes matching the prepared surface to the system being installed, which is why a thin coating and a thicker build ask for different work. A hard, clean slab grinds quickly. A slab with a weak top layer, old coatings, or oil soaked into it takes longer and may need more than one pass.

This step decides whether the floor lasts, so it is the last place to look for savings. Our garage floor preparation guide walks through it zone by zone.

Repairs are counted, not guessed

Cracks, chips, and pitted areas are filled before coating. Cosmetic cracks are common and are part of normal preparation. Wide cracks, broken edges at the door, and areas where salt has scaled the surface take more material and more time.

Some damage only shows up once the floor is ground, because an old finish or dirt was hiding it. A clear quote says how newly exposed damage is handled: included up to a point, priced per item, or reviewed with you before work continues. Ask which one applies.

Moisture can change the plan

A coating sits on top of the concrete. If moisture moves up through the slab, it pushes against the coating from below. Most garages are fine, but a damp corner, white powdery deposits, or a history of water in the garage are worth raising early.

Moisture is measured, not judged by eye. ASTM F2170 is one of the standard tests for relative humidity inside a slab. If a test is needed, or the result calls for a different system, the cost changes. It is better to know that before the floor is coated than after.

The system and the finish

A garage floor system is built in layers. In a flake floor, that means a base coat, a broadcast of flake into it while it is wet, a scrape once it has set, and a clear top coat. An anti-slip top coat is an option where more grip is wanted.

Within a system, three choices move the price:

  • Flake coverage. A full broadcast, where flake covers the whole floor, uses more flake than a partial scatter over a coloured base. It also hides more.
  • Finish family. Flake, solid colour, and metallic floors use different materials and different amounts of hand work. Metallic floors are poured and worked by hand, so they take more time on the floor. Our finish comparison covers the trade-offs.
  • Top coat. The top coat takes the wear, the hot tires, and the cleaning. Ask what it is, and whether an anti-slip version is included or optional.

Access, clearing, and the calendar

A floor has to be empty to be coated. If the garage is full, someone has to move and store what is in it, so ask whether that is you or the crew. A freezer, a workbench fastened to the floor, or shelving bolted to the slab each need a plan.

Timing matters too. A typical garage install takes about two days, and the concrete and the weather can change that. Cold weather slows how coatings cure, so a detached garage in January is a different job from an attached, heated one. The cold-weather guide covers what changes.

How to read two quotes side by side

Quotes differ most in what they leave out. Before comparing totals, line up the scope:

  • How the floor is prepared, and whether the whole floor is ground or only parts of it.
  • Which repairs are included, and how damage found during grinding is handled.
  • The coating system, layer by layer, and the product documents behind it.
  • The finish: flake coverage, colour, and the top coat.
  • How edges, the step, drains, and the strip under the door are finished.
  • Who clears the garage, and when you can park on the floor again.

Alberta’s guidance on hiring a contractor recommends getting estimates, and any changes to them, in writing. That holds for a floor too. Our proposal comparison guide goes further.

Questions people ask

Why is there no price per square foot on this page?
Because the concrete sets the price as much as the size does. Two garages of the same size can need very different grinding, repairs, and moisture checks. A number without seeing the slab would be a guess, so the estimate comes from photos or a visit.
Does a full flake floor cost more than a partial flake floor?
A full broadcast uses more flake, because flake covers the whole floor instead of scattering over a coloured base. It also hides dust and small marks better. Ask for both options on the same quote so the difference is clear.
Can I lower the cost by doing the preparation myself?
Clearing and cleaning out the garage helps. Grinding, repairs, and moisture checks are different: they decide whether the coating bonds, and they need the right equipment and dust control. Preparation is the last place to cut.
How do I get an estimate for my garage in Edmonton?
