Warehouse Floor Coating Planning

Plan warehouse floor coatings in Edmonton around forklift traffic, line markings, diamond-grind prep, downtime phasing, and maintenance for industrial.

Updated
September 18, 2026

Ask Denis about your floor

A warehouse floor project succeeds or fails at the interfaces: a forklift crosses a phase line, a loading door cools the slab, racking conceals damaged concrete, or operations reopen before cleaning and traffic restrictions are satisfied. Product selection matters, but the operating map comes first.

For local scope options, see our commercial epoxy flooring.

Spend one shift observing movement

Mark forklift and pallet-jack routes, turn points, battery or fuel areas, staging lanes, pedestrian crossings, dock doors, exits, fire equipment, drains, racking, and fixed machinery. Record wheel types and loads through the qualified facility team rather than estimating. Add impact zones, leaks, wash areas, de-icer entry, and where grit collects.

Walk again when the warehouse is quiet. Look for joints, cracks, spalls, rocking patches, old coatings, line-marking layers, damp areas, and different concrete placements. Photograph each finding with a plan location. A coating cannot stabilize moving concrete or redesign unsafe traffic.

Keep safety ownership visible

The Government of Alberta’s official OHS Act, Regulation and Code provides current workplace legislation and search tools. Use it to confirm applicable duties for hazard assessment, contractors, access, worker protection, and the tasks involved. The warehouse remains responsible for its operational controls; the contractor remains responsible for the work within its control.

Define barriers, signs, emergency routes, spotters if required by the site, daily handoff, and who can release a phase. Employees should not improvise shortcuts through preparation dust, cords, hoses, uncured material, or abrupt edges. Coordinate fire protection, security, and building management before isolation begins.

Divide the floor by exposure and downtime

Not every zone needs the same visual finish or project sequence. A pedestrian aisle, high-turn forklift corner, storage bay, dock apron, and chemical-handling area may have different exposures. Ask how the documented system addresses each and which conditions need other controls such as curbs, trays, guards, drainage, or sacrificial protection.

Phasing keeps some operations running but adds edges and mobilizations. Draw phase boundaries away from heavy turns where possible, specify how each edge is terminated and later prepared, and protect completed areas from cross-traffic. Use the commercial flooring downtime planning guide to build a handoff sequence.

Preparation must include the inaccessible places

A machine may cover open aisles but not edges, beneath low racking, around columns, or beside drains. The scope should name the preparation method for each. Identify who moves stock and mobile equipment and what remains fixed. Unknown old coatings, adhesive, oil, and weak repairs need a documented investigation process, not a generic square-metre assumption.

Check the proposed profile and repairs against current documents for the complete system. Inspection should occur before primer, with photographs or records of exceptions. If preparation reveals unsound concrete or contamination, agree who stops work and prices the change.

Indoor air and dust cross phase lines

Dust recovery does not eliminate the need for isolation, housekeeping, and equipment maintenance. Product vapours and odours can also travel through doors and shared air systems. Collect safety data sheets for repairs, primers, coats, cleaners, and joint materials.

CCOHS’s official indoor air quality publication frames indoor-air concerns around sources, occupants, building systems, investigation, control, and communication. Use that approach with the site hazard assessment, applicable Alberta requirements, and product SDSs. Name who monitors controls and communicates with adjacent operations.

Markings belong in the layer sequence

Traffic lines, pedestrian routes, location boxes, and safety markings should be coordinated before coating. Identify required colours and layouts through the facility’s competent safety and operations team. Ask whether lines are beneath or above the final coat, how the marking product is made compatible, and how future changes will be handled.

Avoid treating a fresh line as the only traffic control. Routes also need lighting, visibility, rules, training, barriers where required, and maintenance. At handover, inspect colour contrast and continuity in actual warehouse lighting.

Reopen by load, not by one vague date

Written manufacturer restrictions may distinguish pedestrian access, light rolling traffic, loaded forklifts, chemical exposure, washing, and normal service. Temperature and substrate conditions influence the applicable schedule. Loading-door zones in Edmonton can differ from central heated areas, so required condition records should represent each phase.

Plan the first shift: remove barriers only after authorization, confirm routes and signs, inspect for defects, use approved cleaning tools, and prevent sharp turns or parked loads if restrictions still apply. Keep a contingency for repairs or environmental delays.

Compare quotations with an operations checklist

Each proposal should identify zones, measured areas, exclusions, preparation, edge work, repairs, joints, layers, texture, markings, ventilation, inspections, phase boundaries, protection, maintenance, and staged traffic. It should name responsibilities for moving goods, racking access, electrical service, waste, shutdowns, and concealed conditions.

Ask for current product and maintenance documents, not a resin category and a warranty headline. Verify that the specified system relates to the traffic and exposures documented during the walk-through.

Keep a floor asset record

At closeout, retain the zone plan, preparation and repair notes, product data, approved sample, environmental records, line-marking layout, cure release, and maintenance instructions. Assign inspections to an operational role. Photograph turning lanes, dock edges, joints, chemical zones, and phase lines at agreed intervals and after incidents. If routes, loads, wheels, cleaners, or stored materials change, ask whether the original system and traffic controls still fit. This record supports earlier repairs and prevents future crews from treating an identified layer sequence as an unknown coating.
