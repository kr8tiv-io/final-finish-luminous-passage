Repair Mortars Before Coating

Understand concrete repair mortars before Edmonton floor coatings: compatible patches, diamond-grind prep, and recoat timing that fit the selected epoxy system.

Updated
September 18, 2026

Ask Denis about your floor

A repair mortar replaces missing or removed concrete so the floor again has the shape, support, and surface needed for its next use. It is not automatically a crack filler, moisture barrier, levelling compound, or structural design solution. The correct role depends on the defect and on what will be installed above it.

For local scope options, see our concrete preparation.

ACI’s repair guide treats condition evaluation, repair strategy, material selection, surface preparation, application, and quality control as one process (ACI PRC-546-23). Skipping directly to a bag or cartridge reverses that sequence.

Four jobs that are often confused

Product role Typical purpose Question before coating
Patching or repair mortar Rebuild a spall or removed unsound area Is the placement depth, edge detail, and loading suitable?
Resurfacer or overlay Renew a larger shallow area What substrate profile and minimum thickness are required?
Crack repair material Treat a crack under defined movement and moisture conditions Is the crack active, structural, wet, or an intentional joint?
Underlayment or leveller Correct elevation or flatness before a finish Is it approved for the traffic and bonded coating system?

One material may serve more than one role, but only its current product data establishes that. The project should name the role rather than use “repair compound” as a catch-all.

Selection starts with the repair geometry

Measure the defect after unsound concrete is removed, not before. Record depth, area, edge shape, slope, exposure, and whether reinforcement, joints, drains, or moving cracks are involved. A thin feather edge, deep pocket, vertical face, and broad resurfacing area impose different placement demands.

ICRI 320.2R provides guidance for selecting and specifying materials for repair of concrete surfaces (ICRI 320.2R). A useful submittal translates that general selection task into project-specific answers:

  • permitted minimum and maximum thickness per lift;
  • bond or substrate-conditioning method;
  • working time at expected material and site temperatures;
  • shrinkage and movement considerations;
  • compressive and flexural properties where relevant to service;
  • moisture condition permitted during placement;
  • cure and protection requirements;
  • time and condition for overcoating;
  • compatibility with the named primer and coating.

Do not choose by compressive-strength number alone. A repair also has to bond, fit the geometry, tolerate the placement conditions, and accept the next layer.

The repair sequence

1. Confirm the cause and boundary

Map the visibly damaged area, then determine whether adjacent concrete is weak or delaminated. If corrosion, structural movement, repeated water entry, or broad deterioration is suspected, obtain the appropriate professional assessment before defining a cosmetic patch.

2. Remove unsound material

Establish sound concrete and a repair shape that the selected product allows. Protect reinforcement, embedded services, joints, and adjacent finishes. ICRI 310.2R explains that surface-preparation methods differ in capabilities, operating requirements, and limitations (ICRI 310.2R). The method should suit the defect without creating avoidable microdamage or polishing the bond surface.

3. Prepare and condition the substrate

Remove dust, debris, oil, and bond-inhibiting residues. Apply water conditioning, primer, or bonding agent only as the repair manufacturer specifies. “Clean and dry” is not universal; some cementitious materials call for a particular saturated-surface-dry condition, while resinous repairs may require a dry substrate.

4. Batch, mix, place, and finish

Record lot numbers and mixing quantities. Respect water or component ratios, mixing equipment, working time, lift thickness, consolidation, and finish. Adding water to extend working time or spreading beyond permitted thickness changes the installation from the documented product procedure.

5. Cure and protect

The repair may require moisture retention, temperature control, protection from traffic, or a specified wait before grinding and coating. Coordinate curing compounds carefully: a surface-applied cure may conflict with the later bond unless the system documents how it is removed or approved.

6. Verify before concealing

Inspect edges, soundness, level, surface texture, cracks, and cure condition. Confirm the overcoat requirements for both the repair and the coating. Keep photographs because the repair will soon be hidden.

Compatibility has two directions

The repair mortar must bond to the old concrete, and the coating must bond to the repair. Ask both manufacturers’ current documents to answer:

  • Can the coating be applied directly, or is a primer required?
  • What repair age, moisture condition, and surface profile are acceptable?
  • Must laitance or curing residue be mechanically removed?
  • Does the repair contain polymer or other ingredients that change preparation?
  • Are moisture tests interpreted differently over the repair and original slab?
  • What happens at the edge where new mortar meets old concrete?

A product described as “rapid” may gain handling strength before it reaches the condition required for a specific coating. Use the written overcoat criteria, not a generic clock.

Price uncertainty honestly

Preparation often reveals a larger repair than the visible spall. A sound quote identifies the included repair quantity and a controlled mechanism for extras: unit rate, allowance, photographs, owner approval, and schedule effect. That protects both parties from either burying deterioration or treating every newly found condition as an undefined change.

The closeout record should contain the defect map, removal limits, repair product and lot, placement conditions, mixing notes, cure period, preparation before coating, and inspection result. That record is the practical value of a repair mortar: not merely filling a hole, but creating a documented transition from damaged concrete to a substrate the finish system is designed to accept.

Repair around reinforcement, structural cracks, or widespread deterioration requires qualified design. Always follow current repair and coating manufacturer instructions and applicable safety requirements.
