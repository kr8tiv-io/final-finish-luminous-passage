Concrete Sealer vs Floor Coating

Concrete sealer or floor coating? How penetrating sealers, film sealers, and epoxy coatings differ on salt, stains, wear, looks, and recoating in Edmonton.

Updated
September 22, 2026

Ask Denis about your floor

The short answer

A sealer protects concrete from water and salt getting in. A coating puts a new wearing surface on top. Sealers are thin, mostly invisible, and need reapplying. A coating system, like an epoxy flake floor, is a built-up layer that changes how the floor looks, cleans, and wears. Which one makes sense depends on what the floor faces and how you want it to look.

Three things people call sealer

  • Penetrating sealers soak into the concrete and line its pores. Silanes and siloxanes are the common ones. They repel water and salt brine and leave the surface looking much the same. They are widely used on outdoor concrete such as driveways and walks.
  • Densifiers react with the concrete to harden the surface and cut down on dusting. They are used on bare interior floors and under polished concrete.
  • Film-forming sealers, often acrylic, sit on top as a thin clear layer with some sheen. They wear off under traffic and tires and need reapplying.

The ACI guide to protective treatments for concrete covers these families, and more, and how to choose among them for a given exposure.

What a coating does differently

A floor coating is a system of layers bonded to ground concrete. In a flake floor, that is a base coat, a broadcast of flake, a scrape, and a clear top coat. The result is a new surface: sealed against spills, easy to clean, and in the colour and texture you choose.

The difference shows up in daily use:

  • Stains. Oil and fluids soak into bare or sealed concrete over time. On a coating, they sit on top and wipe up.
  • Wear. Film sealers wear through in the tire paths. A coating is built to take the traffic, and its top coat is chosen for it.
  • Dust. Bare concrete sheds fine dust. A coating stops it.
  • Looks. A sealer keeps concrete looking like concrete. A coating changes the floor.

Salt, winter, and Edmonton garages

Salt brine is the winter enemy of concrete. Alberta Infrastructure’s bulletin on de-icing chemicals explains how de-icers and freeze-thaw cycles damage concrete that stays saturated. In a garage, meltwater pools under the car and salt residue collects along the parking tracks.

A penetrating sealer reduces how much brine soaks in. A coating keeps the brine on the surface, where it can be mopped away. The winter salt guide covers the damage patterns on bare slabs.

Coating over a sealed floor

This is the part that catches people. A penetrating or film-forming sealer that is still on the floor can stop a coating from bonding. If a garage was sealed in the past, the sealer has to come off before coating. Diamond grinding does that, and the ICRI guideline on surface preparation is the reference for preparing concrete for sealers and coatings alike.

Not sure whether a floor was sealed? A simple sign is water beading on the surface instead of darkening the concrete. Tell your contractor what you know, including any product names from past owners.

Driveway, garage, and basement are different jobs

The same word gets used for three very different floors:

  • The driveway is outside, in freeze-thaw and direct sun, and it has to breathe. Penetrating sealers are the usual treatment for exterior concrete, and interior floor coatings are not designed for it.
  • The garage sits between outside and in. It takes meltwater, salt, oil, and hot tires. A sealer slows the damage; a coating changes the surface into something that cleans off.
  • The basement is dry living space. A sealer does little there beyond dust control, while a coating turns the slab into a finished floor.

Upkeep over the years

A sealer is maintenance you repeat. Penetrating sealers are reapplied as their water repellency fades, and film sealers are stripped or topped up as they wear through in traffic lanes.

A coating is maintenance of a different kind: regular cleaning, and in time a fresh top coat where the traffic has dulled it. A top coat can often be renewed without redoing the whole system, if it is done before wear reaches the layers below. The recoat timing guide explains why the timing matters.

When each one is the right call

A sealer can be the right choice when you want the floor to keep looking like plain concrete, when the goal is mainly to slow water and salt, or for outdoor slabs. A coating is the right choice when you want a clean, finished floor that resists stains, dust, and wear, and looks the part. Our comparisons of epoxy and concrete paint and polishing and coating cover the other common alternatives.

Questions people ask

Can you put epoxy over a sealed concrete floor?
Not directly. A sealer still on the surface can stop a coating from bonding, so it is removed first, usually by diamond grinding. If water beads on the floor instead of darkening the concrete, there may be a sealer on it.
Is a sealer enough for a garage floor?
It depends on what you want. A penetrating sealer slows water and salt from soaking in, but it leaves bare concrete that still stains and sheds dust. A coating adds a wearing surface that resists stains and cleans easily.
How often does a concrete sealer need reapplying?
It depends on the product, the traffic, and the exposure. Film-forming sealers wear through first in tire paths and walkways. The product's documents give the expected interval between applications.
Does a floor coating protect concrete from road salt?
A coating keeps salt brine on the surface instead of letting it soak into the concrete, so it can be cleaned away. It still needs regular cleaning through the winter so residue does not sit on the floor.
