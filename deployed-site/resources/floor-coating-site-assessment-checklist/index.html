Floor Coating Site Assessment

Walk an Edmonton floor coating site assessment covering slab condition, oil, cracks, moisture clues, diamond-grind access, and photos worth capturing before.

Updated
September 18, 2026

Ask Denis about your floor

A productive site assessment is a fact-finding walk, not a colour consultation. Its goal is to leave with a floor plan, condition map, service brief, testing plan, logistics constraints, and a list of unresolved questions. Use this checklist to prepare the space and record what the assessor actually sees.

For local scope options, see our concrete preparation.

Bring before the walk

  • Dimensioned floor plan or a sketch with overall measurements.
  • Construction year and slab drawings, if available.
  • History of previous flooring, coatings, adhesive removal, repairs, leaks, flooding, and drainage work.
  • Product names for cleaners, degreasers, chemicals, and processes that contact the floor.
  • Photos taken during wet events, even if the floor is dry today.
  • Operating schedule, shutdown limits, and equipment-move constraints.
  • Owner priorities ranked: appearance, texture, cleanability, downtime, repairability, exposure, or budget.

Do not clean away every clue just before the visit. Remove loose items and obvious trip hazards, but preserve photographs and records of recurring stains, damp areas, and damage.

A. What the floor must do

  • Identify pedestrians, footwear, carts, tyres, pallet jacks, forklifts, workbenches, shelving, and point loads.
  • Record wet processes, tracked snow, salt, oils, fuels, food, acids, solvents, disinfectants, or hot materials.
  • Name the cleaning tools, products, water temperature, frequency, and whether liquids pond.
  • Mark sunlight, open overhead doors, exterior transitions, radiant heat, freezers, ovens, or temperature cycling.
  • State desired colour, sheen, texture, line marking, cove, and decorative variation.
  • Decide which scratches, stains, patches, and colour variation would be acceptable.

Avoid the word “chemical-resistant” without a chemical list. Exposure duration, concentration, temperature, and cleanup determine what evidence the system supplier should provide.

B. Divide the slab into zones

Number rooms or production cells and mark boundaries that may indicate different placements. For each zone, photograph and map:

  • cracks, including width, length, edge offset, water, and previous fill;
  • control, construction, isolation, and unknown joints;
  • spalls, scaling, pop-outs, hollow areas, and weak edges;
  • oil, tyre marks, adhesive, paint, curing compound, and suspected sealer;
  • patches and areas with different colour, hardness, or aggregate;
  • slopes, drains, trenches, low areas, ramps, stairs, and thresholds;
  • wall-floor joints, columns, equipment pads, anchors, and penetrations;
  • areas hidden by fixed equipment or inaccessible during the walk.

ACI’s concrete-repair guide places condition evaluation before repair strategy, material selection, preparation, application, and quality control (ACI PRC-546-23). The assessment should therefore distinguish observed defects from the repair recommendation that comes later.

Mark “unknown” where the floor cannot be seen. The proposal can then price access, exploratory preparation, an allowance, or an approval process rather than inventing certainty.

C. Moisture and water history

  • Ask about roof, wall, plumbing, drain, sump, washdown, and groundwater events separately.
  • Mark mineral deposits, damp joints, dark patches, blistered old coatings, and areas beneath mats or storage.
  • Record whether a below-slab vapour retarder is documented, absent, or unknown.
  • Note recent removal of flooring or coatings and whether HVAC is at normal service conditions.
  • Define the moisture test method, number and distribution of locations, timing, and reporting.
  • Obtain the exact coating system’s published acceptance limit before interpreting results.

ASTM F2170 is a quantitative method for measuring relative humidity within a concrete slab, and its official significance statement notes that the result represents the slab condition at the time of the test (ASTM F2170-19a). A handheld scan, plastic sheet, and in-situ RH probe are not interchangeable simply because all relate to moisture.

Visible water entry is a different problem from a coating-acceptance test. Refer unresolved leakage or drainage to the appropriate qualified party.

D. Preparation feasibility

  • Identify the likely existing layer and obtain records or plan a test area.
  • Confirm whether large grinders or blasting equipment can reach the floor.
  • Check door width, elevator capacity, stairs, floor loading, and overhead clearance.
  • Locate suitable power, including voltage, phase, amperage, and panel access.
  • Plan dust collection, edge tooling, cleanup, and waste movement.
  • Identify noise, vibration, and dust-sensitive neighbours or processes.
  • Select a representative test area with real coating, edge, repair, or contamination.
  • Define the target profile and how it will be verified before primer.

The test area is not a free sample of final colour. It is a controlled way to learn removal rate, tool behaviour, substrate condition, edge quality, and hidden damage before a production schedule is locked.

E. Building and operational logistics

  • Measure entrances and identify a material route that does not cross live operations.
  • Assign secure, temperature-appropriate material storage.
  • Mark HVAC supplies, returns, exhausts, intakes, windows, and neighbouring entrances.
  • Identify fire exits, panels, shutoffs, drains, and equipment that must remain accessible.
  • Plan barriers, signs, security, worker facilities, spill response, and waste storage.
  • List other contractors or employees working nearby.
  • Separate reopening needs for inspection, foot traffic, carts, equipment, vehicles, washing, and normal service.

For Alberta workplaces, project parties should review responsibilities and applicable requirements through the official OHS Act, Regulation, and Code portal (Alberta OHS legislation). A site assessment should flag coordination needs; it is not itself the employer’s hazard assessment or safe-work plan.

F. Questions the assessment must leave answered

By the end of the visit, require a short findings record:

  1. What substrate conditions were observed, and where?
  2. Which areas were inaccessible or remain uncertain?
  3. Which tests or exploratory work are required before a firm specification?
  4. What preparation method and provisional profile are proposed?
  5. What repairs are included, allowed for, or excluded?
  6. Which exact system layers are candidates, and what use data supports them?
  7. What access, environmental, ventilation, and shutdown controls shape the schedule?
  8. Which decisions require an engineer, building specialist, hygienist, manufacturer, or owner?

Turn observations into bid attachments

Issue the floor plan, defect map, photos, service brief, test results, site constraints, and unanswered questions to every bidder. Ask them to return the same layer schedule, preparation standard, repair quantities, assumptions, exclusions, and closeout documents.

An assessment has succeeded when it reduces hidden assumptions. It may conclude that the floor is ready for a defined proposal, that testing or exploratory work comes first, or that water or structural evaluation takes priority. All three are useful outcomes.

This checklist is general planning information. Structural conditions, hazardous materials, water entry, exposure controls, and product specification require competent site-specific evaluation.
