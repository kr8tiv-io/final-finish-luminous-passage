Kitchen and Bathroom Epoxy Floors

Epoxy floors in kitchens and bathrooms: what has to be under them, tile removal, water and grip, cabinets and fixtures, odour, and cleaning in Edmonton homes.

Updated
September 22, 2026

Ask Denis about your floor

Can you put an epoxy floor in a kitchen or bathroom?

Yes, on concrete. Epoxy bonds to prepared concrete, so kitchens and bathrooms on a slab are good candidates: basement suites, walkout levels, and homes with concrete floors on the main level. Kitchens and bathrooms are among the spaces Final Finish coats. The questions are about what is under the current floor, how the room handles water, and how the work fits around a household.

It starts with what is under the floor

  • A concrete slab. The usual candidate. It is ground and prepared like any other floor.
  • Tile on concrete. The tile and its thinset come up first, down to sound concrete. That is demolition, and it should be scoped as its own step.
  • Sheet vinyl or plank on concrete. It comes up, and the adhesive left behind is ground off.
  • A wood subfloor. A wood-framed floor flexes in ways concrete does not, and concrete coating systems are not made for it. That is a different conversation, so raise it early.

Water, grip, and the bathroom

Bathrooms get wet, and so do the people in them. A smooth, glossy floor can be slippery with water on it. An anti-slip top coat adds grip without looking like a rubber mat. The CCOHS guidance on preventing slips, trips, and falls points to the walking surface as a key factor, and a wet bathroom floor is the textbook case. Our slip resistance guide goes further.

Around tubs, showers, and toilets, the coating has to stop cleanly, or run under fixtures that come out for the install. Water should never be able to get under an edge. Ask how each edge will be finished.

Cabinets, fixtures, and other trades

Kitchens and bathrooms are full of things fastened to the floor. Decide early:

  • Do the cabinets and island come out, or does the floor stop at the toe kick?
  • Does the toilet come out? That usually means a plumber to remove and reset it.
  • What about the appliances, the vanity, and the dishwasher?

Coating under removed fixtures gives the cleanest result. Stopping at the cabinets is quicker, but it leaves an edge. Either works if it is decided before the quote.

Odour and living in the house

Coating materials have an odour while they go down and while they cure, and kitchens and bathrooms are inside the living space. Ventilation, the product choice, and where the household stays during the install all matter. Health Canada’s guidance on indoor air quality is plain about it: when renovating, open the windows and ventilate. The odours and ventilation guide covers what to ask before the work starts.

Choosing a finish for the room

  • Kitchen. A metallic floor makes a statement in an open kitchen. A solid colour looks clean and modern. Flake hides crumbs and wear in a busy family kitchen.
  • Bathroom. Flake or solid with an anti-slip top coat for everyday bathrooms, metallic where the look matters more.
  • Mudroom and laundry. Flake with grip. See mudroom floor coatings.

Try them on your own room with the floor visualizer, or browse the finish library. The finish comparison sets out the trade-offs.

Basement suites and rentals

Basement suites are one of the best cases for a coated floor. The slab is already there, the floor is quick to clean between tenants, and there is no carpet or underlay to replace or to hold spills. Check the slab for moisture first, because a coating sits on top of whatever the concrete is doing. The basement guide covers moisture and preparation.

Schedule the work between tenancies if you can. An empty suite is faster to clear, easier to ventilate, and nobody has to live around the install.

Where the coating meets other floors

In most homes the coated kitchen or bathroom meets another floor at a doorway: hardwood, carpet, or tile. Plan those transitions before the quote. A transition strip covers the joint, and the heights on each side should be close enough that nobody trips. Coating to a clean, straight edge under a door is neater than stopping at a ragged line.

Comfort and cleaning

A coated floor is as hard underfoot as the concrete under it. Rugs by the sink and the tub help, and in-floor heat makes any hard floor comfortable. Cleaning is simple: a soft broom or dust mop, then a damp mop with a mild cleaner. Skip harsh abrasives. The cleaning guide has the details.

Planning the job

Send photos of the room, the current flooring, and the fixtures through the quote form, and say which fixtures you would like removed. Final Finish gives free estimates from photos or an in-person visit. More on residential epoxy flooring and basement floor coatings.

Questions people ask

Can an epoxy floor go over tile?
Not as a finished floor. The tile and its adhesive come up, down to sound concrete, which is then ground and coated. Coating over tile would copy every grout line and rely on a bond to the tile's glaze.
Is an epoxy bathroom floor slippery?
A smooth, glossy coating can be slippery when wet. An anti-slip top coat adds grip, and it is a sensible choice for a bathroom.
Can epoxy go on a wood subfloor?
Floor coating systems for concrete are made for concrete, not for wood floors that flex. A wood subfloor needs a different approach, so raise it at the start.
Do cabinets have to come out for an epoxy kitchen floor?
Not always. The coating can stop at the cabinets, or the cabinets can come out so it runs underneath. Decide before the quote, because it changes both the scope and the finished look.
